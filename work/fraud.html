558,211 claims. One provider-level view.

I worked on a pipeline that combines inpatient claims, outpatient claims, and beneficiary information. The labeled data contains 5,410 providers, including 506 labeled positive cases, or 9.35%.

Claims & beneficiaries

Aggregate the operating signal

Provider-level counts, reimbursement summaries, durations, demographics, and chronic-condition indicators turn relational tables into a model-ready profile.

Temporal behavior

Represent monthly patterns

Monthly claim counts and reimbursement trajectories provide means, variability, ranges, slopes, and PCA components. Standard PCA is an adaptation, not a full functional-PCA implementation.

Distributional behavior

Look beyond the average

Quantiles, skewness, kurtosis, and entropy summarize how reimbursement amounts are distributed within a provider's claims.

02 / Modeling & decision costs

A risk score is only part of the decision.

I compared logistic regression, random forest, gradient boosting, and an MLP across five random-undersampling ratios, repeated over ten 50/50 stratified splits: 200 model fits.

Implemented investigation rule

Investigate when weighted expected loss exceeds investigation cost.

K × provider reimbursement × adjusted fraud score > investigation cost

The notebook uses K = 20 and an investigation-cost assumption of 4,000. It rescales scores by the natural-to-sampled class ratio. This is a prior-ratio adjustment, not a measured calibration guarantee.

Ranking

ROC-AUC

I use AUC to compare how models rank positive and negative provider labels, rather than describing it as classification accuracy.

Classification

F1 at a decision threshold

I examine the balance between precision and recall at the operating point. The historical non-cost-sensitive comparison searches for an F1 threshold.

Decision consequence

Modeled total cost

Investigations incur a fixed modeled cost; missed positive cases incur a reimbursement-weighted loss. AUC and cost answer different questions.

Cost estimates depend on the study assumptions. They are not realized savings, deployed screening outcomes, or determinations of fraud.

03 / Results & the next iteration

Keep the result. Strengthen the evaluation.

The notebook reports a best mean ROC-AUC of 0.9458, rounded to 0.946, for L1 logistic regression at RUS-10 across ten repetitions.

01

Separate ranking from operating cost

The best AUC model and the best modeled-cost setting need not be the same. I present them as separate evaluation objectives.

02

Move preprocessing inside each split

The historical notebook fits PCA and scaling before its repeated splits. My next evaluation would fit these steps on training data only to reduce information leakage.

03

Tune thresholds without test labels

The historical F1 comparison searches thresholds using evaluation labels. I would move threshold selection to validation folds and reserve the final partition for measurement.
