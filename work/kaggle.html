01 / Challenge & implementation

A complete learning pipeline, built from scratch.

I worked with 16×16 RGB inputs across ten classes, without pretrained weights. I used different network widths and depths to seek complementary predictions from the same data.

Input decision

I resized images from 16×16 to 32×32 with Lanczos interpolation to suit the CNN input pipeline. Resizing changes the sampling grid; it does not recover lost image detail.

02 / Training & inference

Each technique had a specific job.

These are the choices I implemented and their intended benefits. I have not isolated their accuracy contributions through measured ablations.

01

Broaden the training signal

I implemented CutMix and Mixup to train on mixed examples and labels. Random crops, horizontal flips, and brightness/contrast changes were intended to reduce reliance on exact position and lighting.

02

Control optimization

I used SGD with Nesterov momentum and cosine learning-rate decay for a gradually decreasing update scale. L2 weight regularization and label smoothing were intended to discourage overfitting and excessive confidence.

03

Use the GPU efficiently

I enabled mixed-precision training to reduce tensor memory use and use compatible GPU arithmetic efficiently. I kept the final softmax output in float32 for numerical stability.

04

Learn from confident predictions

I ran two pseudo-labeling rounds, selecting unlabeled competition test samples at confidence thresholds of 0.95, then 0.98. The goal was to add likely labels while limiting noisy targets.

05

Average across input views

I used 15 test-time augmentation passes per model, averaging predictions across random crops and flips to reduce sensitivity to a single input view.

06

Combine probabilities

I combined the three models with weighted probability ensembling before selecting the final class. The historical notebook searches weights on its internal validation set; the evaluation limitations are addressed below.

Implemented / Updated notebook

Evaluation and memory updates

  • I separated training, validation, and a reserved evaluation partition to correct validation contamination in the historical pseudo-labeling workflow.
  • I changed training to run models sequentially to reduce peak GPU memory use.
  • I use normalization statistics computed from training data only.

Why the evaluation changed

The historical pseudo-labeling workflow added the full labeled dataset back into fine-tuning, including examples from its internal validation partition. I therefore do not treat the later internal validation scores, or ensemble-weight tuning on that partition, as independent holdout performance. The updated split design addresses this contamination; it does not replace the recorded competition score.
