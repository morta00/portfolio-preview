01 / The product

From a member’s goal to an actionable next step.

GymBox brings activity, member profiles, and shopping into one mobile experience. BoxAI adds two ways to get guidance: ask a fitness question, or generate exercise and meal suggestions from the profile collected during onboarding.

Conversational coaching

Ask. Retrieve. Respond.

The chat sends a question to the FastAPI service. The default retrieval path supplies relevant fitness material to Gemma before the answer returns to the conversation.

Profile-based recommendations

A plan the interface can use.

Age, sex, height, weight, and goal feed a separate recommendation pipeline. The app displays exercises and meals from a JSON response, including duration, estimated calories, and meal macros.

Member experience

AI within the daily workflow.

Onboarding, activity and profile dashboards, QR membership access, and the in-app shop give the coaching feature a place within the wider gym experience.

03 / Engineering

Two AI workflows. One mobile integration.

I separated knowledge retrieval from recommendation generation so each workflow could return the format the app needs.

Fitness Q&A

Mobile chat → POST /ask → Retrieved context → Answer

  • Prepare the knowledge: split the fitness corpus into 1,000-character chunks with 200-character overlap.
  • Search semantically: embed with all-MiniLM-L6-v2 and retrieve the five closest chunks from a persisted FAISS index.
  • Generate with context: LangChain supplies retrieved material to gemma2-9b-it through Groq. The backend also exposes a dedicated /ask-rag route.

Exercise & meal recommendations

Member profile → POST /recommend → JSON → Mobile cards

  • Map the mobile profile: translate stored onboarding fields into the API’s age, sex, weight, height, and goal schema.
  • Generate a structured plan: a separate Llama-based chain requests exercise and meal objects, with calories and meal macros.
  • Render usable output: the service parses JSON and checks required top-level fields; the mobile screen groups exercise and meal cards and respects the meal-suggestion setting.

Implementation choices

Reusable retrieval index
The backend saves the FAISS index and rebuilds it when the dataset is newer.
Repeated-question caching
Bounded LRU caches reuse Q&A responses for identical questions.
Request handling
Query length limits, profile range checks, prompt-level fitness scope, and basic injection-pattern filtering.
Service diagnostics
/health and /dataset-info report component and dataset availability.

04 / Contribution & outcome

AI engineering that reaches the interface.

I developed the BoxAI service and connected its chat and recommendation endpoints to GymBox. I also contributed mobile screens for member profiles, login and account flows, and shopping as part of the collaborative app development.

The result is a demonstrable mobile integration: members can ask training and nutrition questions, and the app can turn profile-based AI output into exercise and meal cards. The screenshots and recordings show the product experience; both repositories expose the implementation.

Next engineering steps

Evaluate, then harden.

My next priorities are retrieval and answer-quality evaluation, schema validation for every recommendation field, session-isolated conversations, and authenticated backend deployment with rate limits.

These are planned improvements. No measured accuracy, latency, adoption, or health outcomes are claimed.
